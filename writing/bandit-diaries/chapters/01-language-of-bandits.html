Bandit Diaries · Chapter 1

The Language of Bandits

The basic setting, structured and unstructured bandits, and why recommendation systems fit naturally into this framework.

Shirish Chinchanikar

Here, we learn the basic language of bandits and some of their applications.

In a simple setting, imagine that you are standing in front of a casino machine with K slots, or arms, each giving a different random reward. At every turn, you choose one arm to pull, observe its reward, and then decide what to do in the next turn. You repeat this for n turns.

The environment class is the set of all the different kinds of machines you could face. A strategy tells you which arm to pull based on everything you have observed so far.

In unstructured bandits, the arms are unrelated. Learning something about one arm tells you nothing about the others. In structured bandits, the arms are connected in some way, so pulling one arm may also give you information about the rewards of other arms.

Bandit algorithms have many applications. One example is recommendation systems. Netflix must decide which shows, titles, or thumbnails to display so that you are more likely to click on something.

After interacting with you several times, the system learns how you tend to respond to different recommendations and uses this information to choose what to show you next.

Small technical note

The Netflix example is more specifically a contextual bandit problem, because the decision depends on context such as the user, their history, and the item being shown.
