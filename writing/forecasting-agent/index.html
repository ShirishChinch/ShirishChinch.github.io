Building a Conservative Hybrid Forecasting Agent

A second-prize forecasting-agent competition post

Shirish Chinchanikar · 8 min read

For the forecasting-agent competition, I built a system around one core idea: a prediction market is usually a strong prior, not an answer to casually overwrite.

The final agent placed second by combining empirical calibration, small residual models, and LLM-based checks for routing and public evidence. The hard part was not finding information. It was deciding when information was strong enough, fresh enough, and underpriced enough to justify moving away from the market.

The agent asked narrower questions: Has this type of market historically been miscalibrated? Are there fast market signals that justify a small correction? Is there public information that is relevant, credible, and not already reflected in price?

Calibration First

The first layer was a hierarchical calibration table. Historical markets were grouped by sector, subtype, time to close, and five-cent probability bucket. A 40-cent weather contract, tennis contract, and politics contract were not assumed to behave the same way. If a bucket had enough data, the agent estimated a residual rather than replacing the live market price:

adjustment = historical YES rate - historical mean market price
forecast   = live market price + capped adjustment

Sparse empirical tables can look convincing while being mostly noise, so the system used sample thresholds, capped moves, and broader fallbacks when the exact bucket was thin. One useful lesson was negative: a global symmetric calibration curve looked plausible, but a market-disjoint chronological backtest made Brier score worse. Every layer had to earn the right to move the prior.

Small Models, Small Moves

The next layer was a fast linear residual model using bid-ask spread, recent price movement, volume, volatility, open interest, and trade count. It was a ridge regression trained to predict only outcome - market_prior, with output clipped to at most five percentage points. A fast model can notice patterns quickly, but it should not swamp the market.

In backtests, the pure order-flow residual model improved Brier score from 0.03816 to 0.03696, about a 3.1% relative improvement. A separate LLM-context residual model improved from 0.17396 to 0.17152, about a 1.4% relative improvement. These results supported bounded residuals; they did not prove future gains.

Where the LLM Helped Most

The LLM was most useful when keyword logic was too brittle. A politics keyword like bill can appear inside Billie Eilish; words such as game, match, over, and under mean different things across domains. I added LLM route verification for ambiguous cases. The verifier was not allowed to forecast. It only checked whether the proposed template was correct and, if necessary, moved the event into a safer category.

Its second role was judging unstructured public evidence. It checked three things: is the information relevant and directionally useful, is it credible, and is it probably not already priced in? Public information is valuable only if the market has not already incorporated it. I treated confidence above 0.40 as a review threshold, not an automatic trade signal.

Live Evidence Check

Historical LLM decisions were not logged well enough to reconstruct every source and judgment. To show the behavior of the evidence layer, I reran it later on live Kalshi markets. These are demonstrations of the system design, not proof of why any past forecast worked.

The defense supplemental case best captures the intended use. The LLM found credible reporting that funding was being discussed, but official sources showed no formal request or scheduled vote. It did not justify a clean YES or NO call. It was simply worth reviewing.

Takeaways

The strongest version of the agent was not the most aggressive one. Historical data handled calibration, fast models handled small structured residuals, and the LLM handled semantic routing and public evidence. Every move was capped, and the system defaulted to the market prior when evidence was weak. No move was a valid output, not a failure case.

In one line

Market prior first. Bounded deviations later.
